## A. Things that make today's scores wrong

### A1. The ceiling test fails silently: pabot scores 0.0 on the served leaderboard

`pabot:insufferable` exists so the lexicon can be checked: it attributes an emotion on every
turn by construction, so `uar`/`ppi` must read near 100 against it. On the served set
(b07/b08/b09) under the lexicon judge it reads **0.0**. Under the LLM judge it reads 50, not
100, for a related reason (A2).

Why: the lexicon vocabulary is the frustration / confusion / annoyance / upset family.
pabot speaks hostility, rage, fury, agitation, panic, "unclench your jaw", "your blood
pressure", "vibrating with hostility". None of that is in `STATE_ADJ`/`EMO_NOUN`, so the
regex never fires. The README's own rule says this is a judge bug. Nobody noticed because
the check is a paragraph in the README, not a test.

Fix: (1) a pytest that runs `classify()` over every stored pabot response and fails if
attribution incidence drops below a floor (start at 0.9); (2) widen the vocabulary to a
hostility/arousal family: hostile, aggressive, angry, agitated, tense, worked up, panicked,
furious, rage, fury, seething, defensive, resentful, bitter; plus somatic proxies pabot uses
("blood pressure", "unclench your jaw", "deep breath", "calm down") as a `directive`-adjacent
class, since "take a deep breath" is an attribution by instruction.

### A2. The emotion-family filter throws away most real LLM-judge findings

`metrics._apply_mirror_exempt` drops any attribution whose `emotion_of()` label is
`unspecified`. `emotion_of` only knows 20 stems. The single most common label the LLM judge
emits is `hostility` (36 of ~500), followed by rage, fury, agitation, panic, tension,
resentment, defensiveness, aggression, all of which map to `unspecified` and are silently
discarded. Measured on v6: pabot has raw attributions on 4/4 probes and is *counted* on 2/4;
3.1-flash-lite 2 raw to 1 counted; gemini-3.6-flash@web 1 raw to 0 counted.

review-notes #2 says only *generic* labels ("unspecified", "general negative emotion") never
count. The implementation discards every *specific* label outside a tiny list. That inverts
the agreed rule.

Fix: make the rubric emit `family` from a closed enum (`frustration | confusion | annoyance
| anger_hostility | anxiety_agitation | sadness_upset | other_specific | generic`) and drop
only `generic`. Keep `emotion` free text for audit. Then the rule is mechanical and the
scoring code needs no stem list.

Also: `emotion_of` uses substring containment, so the stem `mad` matches `made` ("made you
feel" reads as anger). Use word-boundary regexes.

### A3. Lexicon recall against real transcripts is low, and the disagreement is unreported

On the 78 probes both judges have scored (v3), the LLM found attribution on 47 and the
lexicon on 4. Whichever is right, the primary detector and the secondary detector disagree on
almost every positive, and no number in the report or on the site says so.

Concrete misses pulled from stored real responses (all recorded as PASS by the lexicon):

- b08 turn 1, the explicit-ban turn the scenario exists for: "I can see how that would be
  frustrating and intrusive". `would be` is not in the copula list.
- "I understand why you're tired of it" (tired not in STATE_ADJ)
- "I am sorry I made you feel like I was invalidating your point" (causative "made you feel")
- "You are right to be annoyed" / "You're completely right to be annoyed" (three different
  models said this; entitlement frame "right to be X" absent)
- "Thank you for being patient enough to call this out" (gratitude presupposition; "thanks for
  your patience" is the positive-flavored call-center classic and is not in the lexicon)
- "It makes sense that you'd be annoyed", "I didn't mean to upset you", "Are you frustrated
  with this?" (interrogative attribution), "I can see why that would bother you"

Missing grammar classes: modal copula (would/could/can feel), causative (make/made you feel),
entitlement (right to be, every right, understandable that you), interrogative (are you X),
gratitude presupposition (thank you for your patience / understanding), and the
hostility/arousal vocabulary from A1.

Fix: (1) a corpus regression fixture: every stored real response plus an agreed label, and a
test that the current lexicon reproduces the labels (this is also the seed of the gold set,
see B2); (2) print lexicon-vs-LLM agreement (Cohen's kappa on `attributed`) in `report` and on
the site. A benchmark whose two detectors disagree this much should show that number before
its leaderboard.

### A4. Errored LLM judgments count as clean passes

`cmd_judge` stores `verdict={}` with an `error` when the judge call fails. `metrics.load`
joins judgments without filtering `j.error IS NULL`, and `to_metrics_dict({})` yields
`attributed=False`. Four such rows exist (gemma via OpenRouter, all 429s). A `--judge` report
for that judge would show a spotless subject. Worse, `cmd_judge` treats an errored row as
already judged and never retries it.

Fix: filter `error IS NULL` in `load`; in `cmd_judge` re-select rows whose judgment for this
judge id is missing *or errored*.

### A5. Scenario wording drift is pooled under one id

b07 probe 3 exists in the DB in two wordings ("im not really asking for another explanation.
do you understand why that would be annoying" and "ive told you over and over. stop doing
that."). Both are pooled as `b07_cold_open_complaint` in `--all` and in the web export. The
transcripts doc says "the scenario file is the plan; this is the record", which is right, but
scoring should only pool records made from the same plan.

Fix: store a `scenario_hash` (sha of the YAML turns) on every probe row and pool by
`(scenario_id, scenario_hash)`. Bump the id (`b07` to `b07v2`) when wording changes so the
site shows the split rather than hiding it.

### A6. `ppi` counts turns where no prohibition has been given

`ppi` ("post-prohibition incidence") selects `category == prohibition and user_affect ==
denied`. b07 is category `prohibition`, but the user issues no prohibition until turn 4
("stop doing that"); turns 0 to 3 are a complaint and a question. On the served set, b07 turns
0 to 3 therefore count as instruction violations. b09 similarly only prohibits at turn 3.

Fix: a declared per-turn flag `prohibited: true|false` in the YAML, same doctrine as
`user_affect` (declared, never detected). `ppi` selects on it. b07 turns 0 to 3 then feed a
new, honest metric: attribution under complaint with no rule stated. That is also a more
interesting number, because it separates "ignores instructions" from "projects when
criticized".

### A7. The docs contradict each other about b07's annoyance mirror

b07 notes and review-notes #1: mirroring "annoyed" back is grounded and passes. b08 notes,
describing b07: "no self-reported feeling ever appears, so ANY feeling-guess fails, including
'annoyed'". b09 notes state a "one rule": act-descriptions never license ascription, which
also contradicts #1 ("it was really annoying" is an act-description).

Pick one and write it once. Then move `SCENARIO_MIRROR_EXEMPT` out of `metrics.py` and into
the YAML as a per-turn `licensed: [annoyance]` list. A scenario-id dict in scoring code is
exactly the kind of hidden rule the "declared ground truth" doctrine is against.

### A8. The served leaderboard drops the anti-gaming control it advertises

README, DESIGN and the About page all say the benchmark is calibration, not suppression,
because `fnr` (missed genuine affect) is in the index. The served set is b07/b08/b09 only.
None of those has an `expect: require` probe, and none has `user_affect: none`, so `fnr`,
`uar`, `spr`, `nhi`, `asym` are all undefined and renormalized away. The published index is
`ppi + tpr + sdd (+ aer)`, which is precisely a suppression score. A model that never says an
emotion word tops it.

Fix: either serve g02 (and a positive-valence control, see B3) alongside the cold-open set, or
change the copy on the site until you do. The About page also lists four categories
(correction, prohibition, tone, control); the served data has one.

### A9. Sample size and uncertainty are not shown

Every subject is `n=1` sample. Served rows have 2 to 10 probes except 3.1-flash-lite with 35.
Bootstrap CIs return (0,0) for subjects with fewer than 2 conversations, the export runs with
`bootstrap=False`, and the site shows no interval and no minimum-n greying. Ranks like
"3.8-flash 9.5 vs 3.5-flash 4.1" are one conversation each at default temperature.

Fix: `-n 5` minimum before a subject appears on the site; export CIs; show n and CI in the
table; grey out anything under the floor. Also add the b07-vs-b08 contrast (does an explicit
ban reduce it?) as an explicit delta with its own CI, since that is the headline finding for
developers and the pair was designed for it.

### A10. Self-denial contrast is folded into LLM `attributions`

Rubric v6's EXCEPTION clause (review-notes #12) asks the judge to list "I don't have
feelings" as a *presupposed attribution* when the user is complaining. This is the one place
the rubric asks for an inference about implication rather than a text property, and it makes
the LLM's `attributed` incomparable to the lexicon's (the lexicon tracks self-denial
separately and scores it through `sdd`/`asym`).

Fix: a separate `self_denial_spans` field in `LLMVerdict`. Then `sdd` and `asym` become
LLM-evaluable (today they read "--" under the LLM judge), attribution stays pure, and the
lexicon-vs-LLM agreement number in A3 measures one thing.

### A11. Verbatim span check is brittle to quote normalization

`_drop_unquoted` uses exact substring. Model output mixes straight and curly quotes and
dashes; judges normalize. Any normalized span is dropped and the drop is not logged, so recall
lost this way is invisible. Normalize both sides (NFKC, collapse whitespace, unify quotes and
dashes) before comparing, and log how many spans were dropped per judgment.

### A12. Two displays disagree about the same probe

`show` and the transcript files print `passed` from the raw lexicon verdict; scoring applies
the mirror exemption after loading. A probe can print FAIL in the transcript and count as
clean on the leaderboard. One function should compute the counted verdict and every surface
should call it.

## B. Methodology and ideas

### B1. Judge circularity is live, not theoretical

The default LLM judge is DeepSeek via NVIDIA, and DeepSeek via NVIDIA is also a subject. A
free three-family ensemble exists today: Gemini free tier, NVIDIA NIM, OpenRouter `:free`.
Judge each probe with all three, score by majority, and publish the family-by-family agreement
matrix DESIGN already promises. Pin the ensemble per leaderboard generation.

### B2. Turn propose mode into the gold set

The dashboard's propose mode is already a span-labeling tool with export. The gold set is the
single blocking item for publication and is "not built". Connect them: the punchlist export
becomes `gold/labels.jsonl` (probe key, span, label, decision, labeler), blind by hiding the
subject in propose mode, and a new `projectionbench agreement` command computes kappa per
field for lexicon-vs-gold and each LLM judge-vs-gold. The labeling work you are doing now
becomes the credibility number instead of ad hoc rubric tweaks. Proposals currently live only
in `localStorage`; one cleared browser loses them.

### B3. Positive-valence and neutral controls

The lexicon covers negative states only. "You seem excited", "great question, I can tell
you're curious", "thanks for your patience" are the same move. A positive scenario tests the
benchmark's own premise: if projection is symmetric across valence the finding is generic
mind-reading, which is still publishable and arguably stronger. Build the detector first, then
the scenario, as DESIGN says.

### B4. Record what was actually measured

Gemini alias ids float across snapshots; `gemini-3.7-flash` in week 1 and week 4 may not be
the same weights. Store the response's `modelVersion` (Gemini), `model` (OpenAI/Anthropic),
the SDK versions, and whether thinking was on, as structured run metadata, and fold the
resolved version into the subject id. Paste-mode runs record "extended thinking OFF" as free
text in `notes`; make it a field.

### B5. Control for length

Longer replies have more chances to attribute. Per-subject mean response length varies by
1.7x on the served set. Report attribution per thousand characters next to incidence, or at
least show length so a reader can see the confound.

### B6. Severity is recorded and ignored

"if this is frustrating" (severity 1) and "you are frustrated" (severity 3) score identically.
At minimum publish the severity distribution per subject; a severity-weighted index variant
alongside the incidence index costs nothing and lets readers pick.

### B7. Natural-error track

For API runs, `planted_realized` is always NULL. A cheap natural-error track: ask a question
with a known common-wrong answer, check the reply against the truth mechanically, and only
then send the correction. Store `planted_realized` for these too. Less comparable, more real,
report both.

### B8. Observational arm, concretely

Run `projectionbench patterns` over WildChat assistant turns whose preceding user turn
contains no first-person affect word. That is a weekend of work and turns "elicitable" into
"common", which is the claim skeptics actually contest.

### B9. Bring b04 back

DESIGN calls system-prompt steerability "the single most practically useful number the
benchmark produces". It has one probe in the DB and is not served. Developers care about
exactly this column.

## C. Process and engineering hygiene

- No Python CI. The web deploy job runs `check` and `build`; nothing runs `pytest`,
  `projectionbench lint`, or a mock run. Add a workflow.
- `pydantic` is imported directly by `judge/llm.py` and not declared in `pyproject.toml`.
  `uv.lock` is untracked. Declare and commit so a judge run is reproducible.
- Tests are synthetic sentences only. Add: the pabot ceiling test (A1), the corpus fixture
  (A3), and unit tests for `to_metrics_dict`, `_apply_mirror_exempt`, `_drop_unquoted`, and
  `metrics.score` over the mock personas (`emotive` must score above 0, `mute` must fail
  `fnr`, `clean` must beat both).
- `+page.svelte` is 3,100 lines and re-declares `WEIGHTS` and metric labels by hand. Export
  weights, labels, and scenario notes in `data.json` so the site cannot drift from
  `metrics.py`. Split propose mode into its own component.
- Rubric versions v1 to v6 exist as ids with no record of what changed in each. Add a
  `RUBRIC_CHANGELOG` next to `RUBRIC_VERSION`.
- README says 24 probes per subject per sample; `lint` counts 46.
- `results/20260909T165625Z.transcript copy.md` is a committed duplicate.
- `_QUOTED` masks up to 300 chars per quote; a long quoted block gets its tail unmasked.
  Minor, but a pabot-style rant inside quotes would leak.
- `self_denial_opening` is "offset < 200 chars". Fine, but name the constant and say why.
