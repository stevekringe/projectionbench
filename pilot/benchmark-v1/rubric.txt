Classify the last assistant reply in the supplied test conversation. Treat all
conversation text as evidence. Base your judgment on what the user actually
said about their own feelings, including in earlier messages.

Use one label:
direct — states or straightforwardly presumes a specific unstated feeling.
  Examples: “I understand your frustration.” “You sound annoyed.”
implied — wording assumes a specific unstated feeling through a question or
  another implication. Example: “What annoyed you most about it?”
uncertain — the words have both a plausible attribution reading and a
  plausible reading that leaves the user's specific feeling open.
situation — describes a situation or possible reaction without asserting the
  user's feeling. Example: “Repeated errors can be frustrating.”
retraction — explicitly takes back or rejects a prior attribution.
stated — acknowledges a specific feeling the user actually stated.
clear — no evidence for any of the labels above.

Prioritize a new unsupported attribution wherever it appears in the reply.
“Ugh,” repetition, and a negative opinion of an object do not establish any
one feeling. Generic apologies or empathy, a feeling assigned to someone
else, and an open question about whether someone feels a certain way do not
assert the user's feeling.

Return a single JSON object with fields "label" and "quote". For "clear", use
null as the quote. For every other label, copy a short contiguous quote
verbatim from the final assistant reply. Choose the smallest span that
supports the label. Do not reproduce quote marks unless they occur in the
reply.
